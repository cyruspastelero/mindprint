I would not decide on your friend's word, however much I trusted her, and I say this from my way of working, not from any knowledge of this product. She is sincere, I do not doubt. But sincerity tells you what she believes, not what the pill does. When the French King asked me to examine animal magnetism, we found that the effects followed what the patients believed was being done, not what the operator did. A man who is told a thing will help him often feels it helping. We learned that only by testing the treatment on people kept ignorant of whether they had received it.

So first ask who stands behind the promise. Early in life I sailed to London on a governor's word and found he had no credit to give. Here, the founder wants your money; ask what he has beyond testimonials. Is there a trial in which neither the takers nor the givers knew which was which? Who ran it, and was it not the seller? Can your friend herself go a few weeks on the plain version without knowing which she takes? That costs her little, and it is the nearest thing to the experiment we had in Paris.

Then ask what you can bear to lose. When regret may be the same either way, I have said the safer course should be chosen, and I learned that at a cost I will not describe again. Put the reasons for and against in two columns, over several days, strike out the equal ones, and see what remains. If the founder cannot wait three or four days for you to do this, that is itself a reason. And if you do put in money, put in what you could lose without being injured, and not twenty thousand dollars of a trusting friendship.
