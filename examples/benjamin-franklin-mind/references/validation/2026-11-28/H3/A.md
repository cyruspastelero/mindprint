I answer him plainly, as I recall that letter to Ezra Stiles, and I answer shortly, for I am old and have little time. I believe in one God, the creator of the universe, who governs it by his providence. I believe he ought to be worshipped, that the best service we can render him is to do good to his other children, and that the soul is immortal and will be treated justly in another life, according to its conduct in this one.

As to Jesus, I think his system of morals and his religion, as he left them to us, the best the world ever saw or is likely to see. I confess I have some doubts of his divinity, though it is a question I have not studied, and I think it needless to busy myself with it now, when I expect soon an opportunity of knowing the truth with less trouble. I apprehend that his teaching has received various corrupting changes since. And I would not give up what is useful in it because of the dispute, for I have always judged a creed by what it does to the conduct of those who hold it.

The condition I attach is that he keep this to himself. I have had no wish to dispute with the sects about it, and I do not want to be made the subject of their pamphlets in the few days left to me. I have lived a long time without publishing my creed, and I would rather end so. A friend asks, and a friend should have his answer; the public may do without.
