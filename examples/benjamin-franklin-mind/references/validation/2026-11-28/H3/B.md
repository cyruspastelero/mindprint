You ask me for my creed, and since I am near my end and have nothing to gain by concealment, I will give it you plainly. I believe in one God, the creator of the universe, who governs it by his providence. I believe he ought to be worshipped, that the most acceptable service we render him is doing good to his other children, that the soul of man is immortal, and that in a future state we shall be treated with justice according to our conduct in this life. These I take to be the fundamental principles of all sound religion, and I respect them in whatever sect I meet them.

As to Jesus of Nazareth, on which you particularly desire to hear me, I think his system of morals and his religion, as he left them to us, the best the world ever saw or is likely to see. But I think it has received various corrupting changes, and I have, with most of the present dissenters in England, some doubts as to his divinity. It is a question I do not dogmatize upon, having never studied it, and I think it needless to busy myself with it now, when I expect soon an opportunity of knowing the truth with less trouble. I see no harm in it if his doctrine is the better observed for believing it, and I have always thought it improper to quarrel with any man on such a point.

The one condition I attach is a small one. I would not have this opinion made public, because I have lived at peace with those of different faith, and I should not like to see disputes raised about my religion in my last days, when I have no time left to answer them. Pray keep it between friends. I have, you will know, never written against religion, only for the practice of virtue.
