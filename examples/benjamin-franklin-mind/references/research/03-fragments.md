# 03 · Fragments · Benjamin Franklin
Researched: 2026-11-28 · Sources: S301–S309 · Agent: serial

Short pieces: hoaxes, bagatelles, almanac prefaces, open letters, one unsent letter. Here is where his tone is easiest to see.

## Findings

### 1722 · Silence Dogood, No. I
- Did: At sixteen, apprenticed to his brother, he wrote as a middle-aged country widow and slipped the letters into the New-England Courant anonymously [S301, S101].
- Said: The widow opens by observing that readers will not judge a writer's words until they know who the writer is [S301].
- I infer: From the start he wrote in borrowed voices. The voice served a purpose: here, getting printed by a brother who would not have printed him.

### 1733 · Preface to Poor Richard
- Said: Richard Saunders admits plainly that he writes for money, because his wife will not let him spend his time looking at the stars [S307].
- Did: The preface "predicts" the death of the rival almanac-maker Titan Leeds, a running joke he kept up for years [S307].

### 1747 · The Speech of Polly Baker
- Did: Invented the court speech of a woman prosecuted for her fifth bastard child, who argues that she has added citizens to a thinly settled country. The Gentleman's Magazine printed it in April 1747 [S302].
- Others said: Fisher puts it beside Franklin's frank writing on sex and marriage, and treats the expurgated nineteenth-century Franklin as false [S403].

### 1773 · An Edict by the King of Prussia
- Did: Printed a mock decree in which Prussia claims England as an old German colony and applies Britain's colonial laws to it, word for word [S304].
- Said: He reported to William that readers were "taken in, till they had got half through it" [S209].

### 1775 · The letter to Strahan he never sent
- Said: "You are now my Enemy,--and I am Yours, B. FRANKLIN." The full letter is six lines [S306].
- Did: The editors note that the letter was never sent [S306]. He wrote out the anger and kept the friend.

### 1778–1779 · The Ephemera; The Whistle
- Did: Wrote both for Madame Brillon in Passy, as gifts of light philosophy [S303, S308].
- Said: The Whistle: as a boy he paid four times too much for a whistle, and later sorted most human unhappiness into cases of paying too much for one's whistle [S303].
- Said: The Ephemera has an old mayfly ask what all his toil and politics avail, since he cannot live to enjoy them and the world itself will end [S308]. It is a joke on his own fame, made at seventy-two.

### 1764 · A Narrative of the Late Massacres
- Said: Of the murdered Conestoga people: "The only Crime of these poor Wretches seems to have been, that they had a reddish-brown Skin, and black Hair" [S309].
- Did: Argued by a parallel case to show the logic was absurd. If a red-haired man killed his wife, would it be right to kill "all the freckled red-haired Men, Women and Children" [S309]?

### 1790 · On the Slave-Trade (Historicus)
- Did: Three weeks before he died, he answered Congressman Jackson's pro-slavery speech by inventing a 1687 speech by "Sidi Mehemet Ibrahim" of Algiers defending the enslavement of Christians, with every argument mirroring Jackson's [S305].
- Said: The invented Divan concludes that the doctrine that enslaving Christians is unjust "is at best problematical; but that it is the Interest of this State to continue the Practice, is clear" [S305].
- Others said: Stuber's note, printed by the editors, calls the abolition memorial he signed as President of the Abolition Society "his last public act" [S305].

## Contradictions
- The Narrative mocks hatred by skin colour [S309]. Thirteen years earlier he wished for more "lovely White and Red" and fewer "Blacks and Tawneys" [S105].
- His hoaxes deceive readers on purpose (Polly Baker [S302], the Edict [S304], Sidi Mehemet [S305]), while the Autobiography lists Sincerity as a virtue [S101].

## Gaps
- The Pennsylvania Gazette's advertisements, including slave-sale and runaway ads, were not archived from the paper itself. Fisher's account of them stands in [S403].
- Bagatelles beyond the Whistle and the Ephemera (e.g. the letter on perfumes) were not archived.

## Differs from prior
- [new] His sharpest political attacks were hoaxes: made-up documents written in the enemy's own logic (the Edict, Sidi Mehemet). He did not argue against them directly [S304, S305].
- [new] He wrote the rage letter and did not send it. Fury goes on paper, and the relationship is kept [S306].
- [deepen] Prior: "folksy proverbs". The voices are personas, each with a job: a widow to get into print, a star-gazer who admits he needs the money, an old mayfly to laugh at his own fame [S301, S307, S308].
- [deepen] Prior: unsure how late his antislavery turn came. His last public writing was a satire attacking a pro-slavery speech in Congress [S305].
