# 06 · Timeline · Benjamin Franklin
Researched: 2026-11-28 · Sources: S601–S603, plus dates from S101, S201–S210, S305, S404, S501–S506 · Agent: serial

Dates are from the editors' chronological table [S602] unless another source is tagged.

## Findings

### Key dates
- 1706: born in Boston, 17 January (6 January 1705, Old Style) [S602].
- 1718: apprenticed to his brother James, printer [S602].
- 1722: Silence Dogood letters [S301].
- 1723: breaks the secret indenture and leaves for Philadelphia [S101].
- 1724–26: London, stranded by Keith's empty promises; works as a compositor; prints the 1725 Dissertation [S101, S103].
- 1728: Articles of Belief; the Junto [S104, S101].
- 1730: takes Deborah Read to wife; William born around then [S403].
- 1731: library subscription [S101].
- 1733: first Poor Richard [S307].
- 1736: son Francis dies of smallpox [S101].
- 1748: partnership with David Hall; leaves day-to-day printing [S101].
- 1750: letter to his mother about selling an enslaved couple [S404].
- 1751: Observations Concerning the Increase of Mankind; hospital bill [S105, S101].
- 1752: the kite experiment (the editors' table calls it "alleged"); raises a lightning rod over his own house in September [S602].
- 1754: proposes the Albany Plan of Union [S602].
- 1755: Braddock's wagons and the defeat [S101].
- 1757–62: first London mission. 1760 letter to Deborah from London [S603].
- 1764: Paxton march; the Narrative [S210, S309]. Leaves for London in November [S602].
- 1765: Stamp Act. Names Hughes as distributor [S506].
- 1766: Examination before the Commons, February [S201].
- 1772: moral algebra letter to Priestley [S202].
- 1773: Rules for a Great Empire; Edict of the King of Prussia [S110, S304].
- 1774: Deborah dies in Philadelphia while he is in London [S403, S404].
- 1775: returns to Philadelphia; the unsent Strahan letter, July [S306].
- 1776: answer to Lord Howe, July. Sails for France late in the year [S503, S602].
- 1778–85: Passy. Bagatelles (Ephemera, Whistle, Gout) [S308, S303, S203].
- 1784: animal magnetism commission. Letter to William, August. Letter to Vaughan on luxury [S502, S504, S108].
- 1785: returns to Philadelphia. Jefferson succeeds him in France [S601].
- 1787: Convention. Speech on salaries, 2 June. Final speech, 17 September [S206, S205].
- 1789: Abolition Society address, November [S501].
- 1790: letter to Stiles, 9 March. Historicus on the slave trade, 23 March. Dies 17 April [S208, S305, S602].

### Shifts in thinking
- On religion: deist pamphlet (1725) → "not very useful" → private liturgy (1728) → in old age, a public stance of tolerance toward all sects [S103, S101, S104].
- On Britain: imperial loyalist (Albany Plan 1754; 1765 advice to Hughes) [S602] → reluctant agent for repeal (1766) → satirist of the ministry (1773) → enemy (1775–76) [S506, S201, S110, S306, S503].
- On slavery: owner and seller (1750) → household slaves in London (1757–75) → Abolition Society president (1787–90) and satirist of pro-slavery arguments (1790) [S404, S501, S305]. Waldstreicher disputes that this was a real conversion [S406].
- On race and immigration: the 1751 passage on "Palatine Boors" and "purely white People", later cut [S105] → the 1764 Narrative mocking revenge by skin colour [S309] → the 1784 Remarks on "savages" [S107].

### Others' view of him at the end
- Jefferson in 1791: when people asked if he replaced Franklin, he answered "No one can replace him, sir; I am only his successor." [S601]
- Jefferson: "there appeared to me more respect and veneration attached to the character of Dr. Franklin in France, than to that of any other person in the same country, foreign or native" [S601].

## Contradictions
- He was abroad for about 25 of his last 33 years (1757–62, 1764–75, 1776–85), while Deborah and then Sarah ran the Philadelphia household [S602, S404]. His letters show affection for home [S603]; his life shows he chose to stay away.

## Gaps
- The editors' table was used for dates. Exact days were not cross-checked against the Yale Papers of Benjamin Franklin, which could not be fetched.
- No last-twelve-months track applies (died 1790).

## Differs from prior
- [deepen] Prior: unsure whether he was in England when Deborah died. He was, and had been away for ten years [S403, S404, S602].
- [new] His antislavery public acts all fall in his last three years, after decades of owning slaves. The key dates are 1750, 1787 and 1789–90 [S404, S501, S305].
- [deepen] The turn against Britain was late and stepwise. He still urged loyalty in 1765 [S506].
